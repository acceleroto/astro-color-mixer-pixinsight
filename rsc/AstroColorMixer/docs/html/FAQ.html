Astro Color Mixer FAQ & Practical Guide

A field guide for nonlinear RGB color and luminance refinement in astrophotography.

What is Astro Color Mixer?

Astro Color Mixer is a nonlinear RGB finishing tool built around practical astro color bands, luminance targeting, mask visibility, preview diagnostics, and sequential refinement passes.

Where does it fit in a PixInsight workflow?

Use it after calibration, integration, background correction, color calibration, and stretch. It is a post-stretch refinement tool, not a linear calibration tool.

What kind of image should I use?

Use nonlinear RGB images. Both stars-present and starless workflows are supported.

What is Stars Present vs Starless?

Stars Present uses more conservative protection around bright stellar structures. Starless allows more freedom when stars have already been removed. It does not remove stars.

What is the basic workflow?

  1. Open a nonlinear RGB image.
  2. Choose Image Type.
  3. Start with Base Pass.
  4. Make broad H/S/L adjustments.
  5. Use diagnostics to understand the image.
  6. Refine Width and Feather.
  7. Use Range Mask for luminance-targeted work.
  8. Add targeted Refinement Passes.
  9. Inspect preview and masks.
  10. Apply to New Image and save an Adjustment Set if needed.

What are the color bands?

  • Red / H-alpha
  • Orange / Dust & Galaxy Cores
  • Yellow / Warm Stars
  • Green / Cast Control
  • Cyan / OIII
  • Blue / Reflection Nebula
  • Purple / Violet Cleanup
  • Magenta / Halo Cleanup

What do Hue, Saturation, and Luminance do?

Hue rotates color direction, Saturation scales color intensity, and Luminance brightens or darkens the active color region inside the current pass.

What are Width and Feather?

Width controls how broad the selected hue family is. Feather controls the softness of the falloff into neighboring hues.

What is Selected Band Blur?

Blur is a spatial blur control for the active selected-band mask. It is different from Feather: Feather softens hue-distance falloff, while Blur spatially smooths the final mask across neighboring image pixels.

Blur is only active in Starless mode. It is disabled in Stars Present mode because spatial mask blur can bleed adjustments into star cores, halos, and nearby structures. Use it for starless color-mask edge cleanup, starting with 1 px and increasing only as needed up to 5 px.

What is Range Mask?

Range Mask targets a luminance interval using Low, High, and Feather controls. It helps isolate faint signal, background, highlights, bright cores, or stars within the active pass.

What is Neutral / Low-Saturation?

It is the luminance adjustment path for weak-color pixels where hue is unreliable, such as sky background, gray dust, and low-color halos.

Why might a low-saturation galaxy not respond strongly?

Some broadband galaxies have real but weak color in outer arms, dust, and faint halos. Protect Low Sat may reduce slider response in these pixels because low saturation makes hue less reliable.

Turning off Protect Low Sat can reveal faint galaxy color, but it can also create blotchy color, noisy hue patches, and colored star halos. The safest workflow is starless data, mask inspection, and multiple smaller passes rather than one extreme pass.

What are Refinement Passes?

They are sequential adjustment passes. Base Pass handles broad work; later passes handle targeted work. They are not Photoshop layers.

What does the probe do?

The probe samples a preview pixel, reports luminance, hue, and saturation, places histogram and polar markers, and can auto-select the nearest band when hue is reliable.

What are the histogram and polar plot showing?

The histogram shows preview luminance distribution and Range Mask placement. The polar plot shows hue angle and saturation distribution.

What are mask views?

Mask views show selected band, Range Mask, or combined influence. White means included more strongly; black means excluded more strongly. In Starless mode, Current Band Mask and Combined Mask reflect active Selected Band Blur.

Why can preview differ from final output?

Preview is downsampled for speed, while Apply to New Image processes full-resolution source data.

What are Adjustment Sets?

Adjustment Sets are JSON files that store passes, sliders, Selected Band settings, selected-band Blur values, Range Mask settings, and related working state.
